\title{A generative surrogate of MINERvA reconstruction\\ trained on the open-data Monte Carlo: performance report}
\author{L.~Wan\thanks{lwan@fnal.gov}}

\begin{abstract}
We describe and evaluate a per-event surrogate of the MINERvA reconstruction chain, trained on the open-data
Medium Energy FHC Monte Carlo. Given a generator's final-state particles and the interaction vertex, the model
samples whether a muon candidate is reconstructed, the MINOS match and charge, the reconstructed muon momentum,
vertex and calorimetric energies, and a variable-size set of hadron prongs, and writes them as a pruned
MasterAnaDev ntuple. On a held-out subrun split, gradient-boosted classifiers trained to tell surrogate from
MasterAnaDev output reach AUC \nAUCmarg{} on the event-level reconstructed quantities and
\nAUCprong{} on the prong content, with and without the truth information as additional input. All
one-dimensional marginals agree to a Wasserstein distance below 0.06 in standardised units. We document the
remaining discrepancies and the parts of the tuple that are derived rather than generated.
\end{abstract}

\section{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Data.} MINERvA open data~\cite{opendata}, Medium Energy FHC standard MC, playlist 1A. The
\texttt{Truth} tree holds every generated interaction and the \texttt{MasterAnaDev} tree those with a
reconstructed muon candidate, together with a copy of the truth. The training population is CC~$\nu_\mu$
interactions with true vertex $z \ge 4000$\,mm; neutral-current, $\nu_e$ and antineutrino events are out of
scope for this version. \input{../m3_1A_z/tables/data.tex}

\paragraph{Inputs.} GENIE final-state particles after intranuclear FSI (\texttt{mc\_FSPart*}), dropping
neutrinos, GENIE pseudo-particles and nuclear remnants, and hadrons with kinetic energy below 10\,MeV. Neutrons
are kept. Each particle enters as a species class (12 classes) and its momentum $(p_x, p_y, p_z)$; the context is
the true vertex $(x,y,z)$ and the struck nucleus $(Z, A)$. Generator-internal labels (interaction mode, $Q^2$,
$W$, $E_\nu$) are deliberately not used, so that the surrogate depends only on what physically enters the
detector and can be applied to final states from any generator.

\paragraph{Splits and metrics.} Events are split by MC subrun into train, validation and test; every number
below is on the test split. Two kinds of metric are used throughout. For one-dimensional distributions we quote
the median with the 16--84\% interval for MasterAnaDev and for one surrogate sample per event, and the
1-Wasserstein distance $W_1$ between them. For closure we train a gradient-boosted classifier
(\texttt{HistGradientBoostingClassifier}) to separate MasterAnaDev from surrogate output on half of the test
events and quote its AUC on the other half: 0.5 means indistinguishable. The classifier is run on the
reconstructed quantities alone (a marginal test) and on the reconstructed quantities together with 48 summary
features of the true final state (a conditional test); the latter is sensitive to whether the surrogate's
response is right \emph{given} the truth, not only on average.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}

Figure~\ref{fig:schematic} shows the architecture. One transformer set encoder over the particle tokens, with a
global token carrying the context, produces an event embedding $z$ and per-particle states $h_i$; every other
component is a head on these outputs, and the whole model is trained jointly with teacher forcing (each head
sees the true values of the quantities it is conditioned on). At sampling time the heads are hierarchical: the
hadron multiplicity $N$ is drawn first, and the prong kinematics are then generated for exactly $N$ prongs. The
prong generator is itself a second, smaller transformer over the $N$ prong tokens, so the model is not a single
transformer with output layers but an encoder followed by MLP heads, an MLP-based flow and a set-transformer
flow. The four groups of heads, in sampling order, are: (i) classification heads for the reconstruction flag, the MINOS match,
the muon charge, the prong multiplicity $N$, and two zero flags stating whether the non-vertex energy within
100\,mm and the isolated-blob energy are exactly zero (Section~\ref{sec:zero}); (ii) a vertex-plane head, because the reconstructed vertex $z$
lies exactly on one of about 200 scintillator-plane positions 22\,mm apart for \nSnapReal{} of events, which predicts
``not snapped'', ``snapped to the plane nearest the true $z$ plus $\delta\in[-3,3]$'' or ``snapped to a farther
plane'' from $z$, the flags, $N$ and the phase of the true $z$ within the plane cycle; (iii) an event flow,
conditional flow matching over 9 variables (muon momentum as a response to the true muon, vertex residual, three
calorimetric energies), conditioned on $z$, the flags including the zero flags, and $N$; for an event whose zero
flag is set, the corresponding energy dimension is switched off in the flow (zeroed at the input, excluded from
the loss), so the flow models only the positive part and the sampler writes an exact zero; (iv) a prong set
flow, masked flow matching over $N$ prong tokens of 10 fields (has-kinematics flag, direction, pion- and
proton-hypothesis momenta, proton score, exiting and primary flags; binary fields dequantised), with a
permutation-equivariant velocity network that self-attends over prongs and cross-attends the particle states,
conditioned on $z$, the flags, $N$ and the event-flow sample. A decoder inverts the transforms, snaps the vertex
when the class head says so, and fills the pion, proton and secondary-proton blocks of the tuple through the
prong table. Two calorimetric branches, \texttt{recoil\_passivecorrected} and \texttt{hadron\_recoil}, are
deterministic MasterAnaDev calibrations of \texttt{recoil\_E} with point masses at fixed ratios and are
derived at decode time from a $z$-binned median ratio rather than generated (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).

\paragraph{Training.} The model is trained in two stages, the encoder with the flag, multiplicity and event-flow
heads first and the vertex-plane head and prong set flow added in a warm-started second stage, with AdamW and a
one-cycle schedule. Training batches are length-bucketed: events are shuffled, cut into chunks of fifty batches,
each chunk sorted by token count and split into batches, and the batches shuffled. One event in a hundred has
more than fifty tokens and the longest has 179, so a random batch is padded to about a hundred tokens for a
typical event of five, and the encoder, two thirds of the step, works mostly on padding; with bucketing the
padded length follows the typical event. On one RTX~3090 the two stages take \nZCHoursMtwoNew{}\,h and
\nZCHoursMthreeNew{}\,h, \nZCHoursNew{}\,h per model against \nZCHoursOld{}\,h with random batches, at the
same validation loss.

\subsection{Event classification heads}
\label{sec:heads}

Table~\ref{tab:tier0} gives the test log loss and AUC of the three binary heads against two references: the
constant-rate (marginal) predictor and a gradient-boosted tree trained on the 48 hand-built summary features
of the final state. The surrogate, which sees the raw particle set, beats the tree on every target, most
clearly for the reconstruction flag (\nRecoLL{} versus \nRecoLLtree{}). The predicted
reconstruction probability is unbiased as a function of the true hadron count (Fig.~\ref{fig:heads}, right).
Table~\ref{tab:mult} does the same for the prong multiplicity; the sampled multiplicity reproduces the
MasterAnaDev distribution (Fig.~\ref{fig:heads}, left). Figure~\ref{fig:mult-conf} shows the multiplicity
response as confusion matrices. The two truth-conditioned matrices, $P(N_\mathrm{reco} \mid n_\mathrm{true})$
for MasterAnaDev and for the surrogate, agree to \nConfMaxDiff{} in every cell: an event with one true charged hadron is
reconstructed with no prong \nConfOneZero{} of the time and with one prong \nConfOneOne{}, with four true charged hadrons the most
probable outcome is still a single prong (\nConfFourOne{}), and the surrogate reproduces this saturation. The third matrix
pairs MasterAnaDev's count with one surrogate draw for the same event (Table~\ref{tab:mult-conf}); since
reconstruction is stochastic the two draws need not coincide, and the matrix should be read as a check that the
surrogate's count is correlated with the real one through the truth, which it is, not as an accuracy.

The asymmetry of Table~\ref{tab:mult-conf} deserves a word, because it looks like a bias: events that
MasterAnaDev reconstructed with three or more prongs are given one or two prongs by the surrogate most of the
time, while the reverse rows are nearly diagonal. It is regression to the mean in the statistical sense. Given
the truth $X$, the real count and the surrogate draw are two independent realisations of the same conditional
distribution $p(N \mid X)$ if the surrogate is right; an event that happened to come out with four prongs is,
in most cases, an event for which $p(4 \mid X)$ is small, so a second draw most probably returns to one or
two. Row normalisation then divides very unequal row totals (\nEvZero{} events at $N=0$ against \nEvSixPlus{} at $N \ge 6$)
and produces the asymmetric appearance. Two checks confirm this reading (Fig.~\ref{fig:conf-sym}). First, if the
two draws are exchangeable the \emph{joint} matrix must be symmetric, and it is: the largest asymmetry
$|J_{ij} - J_{ji}|$ is \nJointAsym{} against a largest entry of \nJointMax{}. Second, two independent surrogate draws
for the same events give a row-normalised matrix with the same asymmetry as MasterAnaDev-versus-surrogate,
to within 0.01 in every populated cell; MasterAnaDev re-run with a different random seed would produce the same
picture against itself. The generative model is therefore expected to agree distribution by distribution and
conditional by conditional, which it does (Fig.~\ref{fig:mult-conf}, left and centre), and not event by event.

\subsection{Hadron prongs}
\label{sec:prongs}

Table~\ref{tab:prongs} compares the prong-level marginals over all prongs of reconstructed test events. A
prong either carries kinematics (a direction and a pion-hypothesis momentum, \nKinFrac{} of prongs) or
nothing but its existence; \nPfitFrac{} also have a proton fit. Direction, both momentum hypotheses and the
proton score are reproduced to $W_1 \le \nProngWoneMax{}$ (Fig.~\ref{fig:prongs}), and the per-event
counts of prongs by category to within one per cent (Fig.~\ref{fig:prong-event}). No prong-to-true-particle
correspondence is generated; the flow learns the prong set given the particle set and any assignment lives
implicitly in the cross-attention.

Closure AUCs summarise agreement but hide what the hadron output looks like, so Figures~\ref{fig:prong-cond}
and~\ref{fig:prong-pid} compare physical quantities against the truth. The fraction of events with a proton fit
rises from \nPfitLow{} at a true leading-proton kinetic energy of 50--80\,MeV to \nPfitPeak{} at its peak near 300--400\,MeV and falls again
for protons above 1\,GeV, which increasingly exit or interact; the surrogate follows this curve to within one
per cent (highest bin: \nPfitHiFake{} versus \nPfitHiReal{}). The reconstructed proton-hypothesis momentum of
the leading prong tracks the truth in median and in the 16--84\% band over the same range. The mean number of
reconstructed prongs versus the true number of charged hadrons after FSI (Fig.~\ref{fig:prong-cond}, right)
shows the saturation of the reconstruction: it rises from \nProngsOne{} at one charged hadron to \nProngsFour{} at four and turns
over to \nProngsSix{} at six, and the surrogate reproduces the whole shape, including the separate curve for prongs
that carry kinematics, to within 0.01 prongs above one hadron; at zero true charged hadrons (\nEventsZeroCh{} events,
where every prong is a fake from a neutron, photon or overlay) the surrogate gives \nProngsZeroFake{} against \nProngsZeroReal{}.
Figure~\ref{fig:prong-pid} tests particle identification: the proton score of prongs in events whose leading
true hadron is a proton peaks at zero but has a shoulder at 0.8 that is absent for pion-led events, and the
surrogate reproduces both shapes (medians \nScoreMedPReal{} versus \nScoreMedPFake{} for proton-led, \nScoreMedPiReal{} versus
\nScoreMedPiFake{} for pion-led). The same figure shows the pion-hypothesis momentum in MeV and the prong angle to the
beam in degrees, where the backward population above 90$^\circ$ and the dip at 90$^\circ$ from the planar
tracking are both present in the surrogate.

Because the surrogate produces no prong-to-particle correspondence, particle identification is tested on a
sample where the correspondence is unambiguous: events with exactly one true charged hadron and exactly one
reconstructed prong (\nPIDevents{} MasterAnaDev events). Table~\ref{tab:pid} and
Figure~\ref{fig:pid} give, for each true species, the probability of each reconstruction outcome: no kinematics,
kinematics without a proton fit, proton fit with score below 0.5, proton fit with score at or above 0.5. A true
proton ends with a high proton score in \nPIDprotonHigh{} of cases against \nPIDpionHigh{} for a true $\pi^+$, and the surrogate
reproduces every entry of the matrix to within \nPIDmaxDiff{}. Using the score to separate protons from charged
pions among prongs with a proton fit gives an AUC of \nPIDaucReal{} in MasterAnaDev and \nPIDaucFake{} in the
surrogate (Fig.~\ref{fig:pid}, right), a difference of \nPIDaucGap{}.

\subsection{Vertex plane}
\label{sec:vertex}

\section{Holdout test: a 2p2h-blind surrogate}
\label{sec:holdout}

The purpose of the surrogate is to produce reconstructed-level predictions for final states the released
simulation does not contain. The first test of that claim holds out an entire interaction mode: a second model
(B) is trained from scratch with all 2p2h events (GENIE mode 8, \nHtwoP{} events, 2.8\% of the population)
removed from training and validation, while the model of Sections~\ref{sec:model}--\ref{sec:perf} (A) saw them.
Both are evaluated on the same \nHtest{} 2p2h test events against the open dataset's own reconstruction of those
events; B is also evaluated on the non-2p2h test events as a control. The models use the adopted input
definition of Section~\ref{sec:inputdef}; the same study was first run with the original definition, and that
result is kept below because it is what identified the input problem. Neither model receives the interaction
mode as input, so the test asks whether the detector response learned from QE, resonant and deep-inelastic
final states transfers to 2p2h final states.

\paragraph{Where 2p2h sits in input space.} Figure~\ref{fig:hold-cov} shows what the blind model has to
extrapolate to. After the input cuts, 2p2h final states are pion-less: \nHfracTwoPtwoph{} have exactly two
protons, against \nHfracTwoPother{} of all other modes. The two-proton, pion-less category is nevertheless
populated in training by \nHnTwoPother{} events from other modes, mostly resonant and deep-inelastic events in
which the pion was absorbed, with softer protons (median leading-proton kinetic energy \nHleadKEQE{}\,MeV for QE
and \nHleadKERES{}\,MeV for RES against \nHleadKETwoPTwoH{}\,MeV for 2p2h). The holdout is therefore a strong
shift in composition and a moderate shift in kinematics within covered support, not a test on unseen final
states; it is the realistic case of applying the surrogate to a generator with a different 2p2h model.

\includegraphics[width=0.9\textwidth]{../holdout_2p2h_1A_z/figures/holdout_input_coverage.png}
\caption{Input coverage of the held-out mode. Left: final-state category after the input cuts for 2p2h and for
all other modes. Right: leading-proton kinetic energy in two-proton, pion-less final states by interaction mode.}
\label{fig:hold-cov}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Result.} Table~\ref{tab:holdout} compares the two models. On the 2p2h test events the blind model
is statistically indistinguishable from the model that saw them: closure AUC \nHBAUCmarg{} against
\nHAAUCmarg{} on the reconstructed quantities and \nHBAUCcond{} against \nHAAUCcond{} with the truth features,
prong closure \nHBProng{} against \nHAProng{}, identical log losses for the reconstruction flag and the
multiplicity, and $W_1$ for the recoil energy of \nHBWoneRecoil{} against \nHAWoneRecoil{}. On the control sample
B matches A's full-test performance (\nHCtlAUCmarg{} against \nHFullAUCmarg{}), so removing 2.8\% of the data
cost nothing elsewhere. Figures~\ref{fig:hold-cond}, \ref{fig:hold-eff} and~\ref{fig:hold-mult} show the three-way comparisons: the
muon response, the recoil energy versus hadronic energy and the proton momentum versus the true proton energy
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hold-cond}); the efficiency, the prong count versus true multiplicity and the proton-fit rate
(Fig.~\ref{fig:hold-eff}); and the multiplicity confusion matrices (Fig.~\ref{fig:hold-mult}), with A and B lying
on top of each other everywhere.

\paragraph{What the residual was, and how it was removed.} The holdout was first run with the original input
definition (hadrons above 50\,MeV, neutrons dropped), and both models then differed from the open dataset on 2p2h
by about the same amount (AUC \nHoldAAUCmarg{} and \nHoldBAUCmarg{} against \nHoldFullAUCmarg{} on the full
split). The per-block classifiers located the difference in the calorimetric block (conditional AUC
\nHoldACalo{}) and in fake prongs of events with no true charged hadron, which the open dataset produces 7\% of
the time and the surrogates never did. Both pointed at the neutron that 2p2h emits in about half of its final
states, invisible to a model whose input excludes neutrons. Section~\ref{sec:inputdef} tests this directly:
admitting neutrons as tokens, with the threshold lowered to 10\,MeV, brings the 2p2h closure of model A with
truth features from \nVKEfiftyTwoPAUCcond{} to \nVKEtennTwoPAUCcond{}, the calorimetric block from
\nVKEfiftyTwoPCalo{} to \nVKEtennTwoPCalo{}, the recoil $W_1$ from \nVKEfiftyTwoPWoneRecoil{} to
\nVKEtennTwoPWoneRecoil{}, and reproduces the neutron-induced fake prongs. The remaining 2p2h residual of that
model (\nZCAonheldout2p2hOldM{} / \nZCAonheldout2p2hOldC{}) sat in the isolated-blob energy, the quantity most
sensitive to neutrons, and turned out to be a rendering problem of the flow at the spike of exact zeros rather
than a physics or coverage problem; the zero flags of Section~\ref{sec:zero} remove it
(\nZCAonheldout2p2hNewM{} / \nZCAonheldout2p2hNewC{}), and the blind model follows
(\nZCBonheldout2p2hNewM{} / \nZCBonheldout2p2hNewC{}). The residual was a limitation of the input definition
and of the output representation, not of the extrapolation, and it is closed.

\paragraph{How large is the loss of fidelity, and is it resolved?} The surrogate represents the aleatoric
spread of reconstruction by construction, but it has no epistemic component: one set of weights, no ensemble,
no signal that an input is far from the training support. The fidelity cost of blindness therefore has to be
measured. Three classifiers were trained on the same \nHtest{} held-out events, with a bootstrap over the
classifier's test half for the evaluation error (\nUnBoot{} resamples, 68\% intervals): open dataset against A
reaches AUC \nUDAMarg{} [\nUDAMargLo{}, \nUDAMargHi{}] on the reconstructed vector and \nUDACond{}
[\nUDACondLo{}, \nUDACondHi{}] with the truth features; open dataset against B, \nUDBMarg{} [\nUDBMargLo{},
\nUDBMargHi{}] and \nUDBCond{} [\nUDBCondLo{}, \nUDBCondHi{}]; and A directly against B, \nUABMarg{}
[\nUABMargLo{}, \nUABMargHi{}] and \nUABCond{} [\nUABCondLo{}, \nUABCondHi{}]. Two conclusions follow. The
blind model's output is not distinguishable from the informed model's, while each differs from the data by
about a hundredth in AUC: the fidelity lost by blindness is below what the test resolves, and what remains is
the surrogate's own residual, the same for both models. The run-to-run variation between independent
evaluations (sampling draws and classifier splits) is about 0.01 in AUC, more than the bootstrap intervals of a
single evaluation. Differences below that are not resolved with one seed per model; resolving
them, and obtaining an epistemic flag for extrapolation, requires an ensemble of seeds, which is the deferred
part of this milestone.

\paragraph{Scope of the conclusion.} A single seed per model was trained, so differences below about 0.01 in
AUC are not resolved. The result establishes that the response transfers across a composition shift within
covered support, which is the common case for generator comparisons; it does not establish extrapolation to
final states absent from training, for which the natural next test is to hold out all two-proton, pion-less
events regardless of mode.

\paragraph{Is 2p2h reachable by reweighting?} The value of the holdout as a stand-in for a generator difference
rests on 2p2h populating final-state phase space that a 2p2h-free sample populates sparsely, so that a user could
not simply reweight into it. In the model's input space this is measured directly: a classifier of 2p2h against
all other processes gives, through its odds and the prior, the density ratio of the rest to 2p2h at each event,
which is the weight a reweighting would need there. Table~\ref{tab:coverage} lists, for each process, the
fraction of its events lying where the other processes' density is below a tenth and below a hundredth of its
own (classifier calibrated to within 0.03 up to a predicted probability of 0.99, and cross-checked with a direct
count in a discrete partition of the inputs, which gives 74\% and 18\% for 2p2h). Three quarters of 2p2h lies
where the rest is below a tenth of its density, and about a quarter where it is below a hundredth; the signature
is the exact two-nucleon, no-pion final state, in particular a proton with a single neutron, at low hadronic
energy. Projected onto the two proton momenta alone this is invisible, since the other processes fill that plane
at comparable density: the exclusivity lives in the species counts and the hadronic energy, not in the proton
kinematics. The 2p2h holdout is therefore a test of prediction in a region a 2p2h-free sample cannot be
reweighted into, while every input particle is still an ordinary particle at an ordinary momentum. Quasi-elastic
scattering is more exclusive still and large, and is the natural second, harder holdout.

\section{Input definition: threshold and neutrons}

\section{Zero flags for the calorimetric spikes}
\label{sec:zero}

Two of the generated energies, the non-vertex energy within 100\,mm and the isolated-blob energy, are exactly
zero in six and 35 percent of reconstructed events, and their continuum begins at a hard threshold of a few MeV.
The first version of the model fed the flow a single continuous variable per energy, with the exact zeros
dequantised into a narrow band below the continuum. The marginal test of Section~\ref{sec:bayes} showed what that
costs: the flow exchanged mass between the zero band and the onset of the continuum, under-populating the first
continuum bins by up to a quarter and over-populating the next ones, and the isolated-blob energy was the
worst-rendered quantity of the model and the one that carried the residual 2p2h closure. The present model
replaces the dequantisation by a Bernoulli flag per energy, predicted from the event embedding like the
reconstruction flag and trained with teacher forcing; the two flags enter the flow's conditioning, and for an
event whose flag is set the corresponding flow dimension is zeroed at the input and excluded from the loss, so
the flow models the positive part only. At sampling the flags are drawn, the masked dimensions are filled with
values that decode to exactly zero, and everything else proceeds as before. The flags are marginally exact by
construction and the zero fractions of the surrogate match the open dataset to the third decimal. Inputs are
unchanged (10\,MeV with neutrons).

Table~\ref{tab:zero-compare} compares the previous and the present model on the same tests. The full-split
closure improves by a hundredth on the reconstructed vector and is unchanged with the truth features; the 2p2h
closure improves from \nZCAonheldout2p2hOldM{} / \nZCAonheldout2p2hOldC{} to \nZCAonheldout2p2hNewM{} /
\nZCAonheldout2p2hNewC{}, so the residual the holdout had left is removed; the marginal pulls of the two spike
quantities fall from \nZCPullNonvtxOldCtl{} and \nZCPullBlobsOldCtl{} to \nZCPullNonvtxNewCtl{} and
\nZCPullBlobsNewCtl{} on the control, the prong-count pulls from \nZCPullNprongOldCtl{} to
\nZCPullNprongNewCtl{}, and the muon and recoil pulls are unchanged within their run-to-run variation. What
remains in the blob energy is the hard lower edge of the positive part, which the flow still smooths upward by a
few MeV; the natural cure is to transform the positive part so that the threshold is mapped to minus infinity,
$\log(E - E_\mathrm{min})$ with $E_\mathrm{min}$ just below the physical minimum, which turns the hard edge into
a smooth tail. That change has not been made.

One lesson of the change is recorded because it affects any model-space comparison. The open dataset's exact
zeros are dequantised uniformly over a narrow band in model space, and a first version of the sampler filled
every zero with the band's centre. Both decode to exactly zero, but in model space a point mass against a band
is trivially separable, and the closure AUC read 0.79 and the fine model-space bins pulled at ten before the
sampler was made to fill the band uniformly. Model-space comparisons must treat the exact zeros identically on
both sides.

\section{Epistemic uncertainty from Bayesian last layers}
\label{sec:bayes}

The surrogate as described so far represents the aleatoric spread of reconstruction but not its own
ignorance. To give it an epistemic component without retraining, the final linear layer of every head is
given a Gaussian posterior over its weights while the rest of the network is frozen (Fig.~\ref{fig:schematic},
purple). For the two flows the flow-matching objective is a linear regression of the target velocity on the
last-layer features, so the posterior is the exact Bayesian-linear-regression posterior,
$\Sigma_o = (\lambda I + H^\top H/s_o^2)^{-1}$ per output $o$, with the noise $s_o^2$ from the residual and the
prior $\lambda$ from the Gaussian evidence; this is the optimum a variational Bayesian last layer converges to
with a fixed backbone. For the classification heads the Laplace approximation of the last layer is used. One
pass over two million training events fits all five layers in about a minute. Two epistemic quantities follow
per event: the variance of a head's logit, $h^\top \Sigma h$, and for a generated event-flow sample the
first-order variance of the final state under a weight perturbation held fixed along the trajectory,
$g_o^\top \Sigma_o g_o$ with $g_o = \sum_s \Delta t\, h_s$. Drawing $K$ weight samples and regenerating gives
the epistemic spread of any binned prediction. In words: the reconstruction-flag variance is how unsure the model
is about the probability that this event gets a muon candidate at all; the multiplicity variance, how unsure it is
about the prong-count probabilities; the event-flow variance, how much the generated muon, vertex and calorimetry
vector would move if the output weights were re-drawn from their posterior. All three are properties of the
input event, available without any truth or comparison model.

\paragraph{Does the flag detect the held-out mode?} No, and it should not. Fitted on the 2p2h-blind model B,
the per-event variances on the \nBayBnOOD{} 2p2h test events separate from those on \nBayBnCtl{} in-distribution
control events with AUC \nBayBExistAUC{} (reconstruction flag), \nBayBCardAUC{} (multiplicity) and
\nBayBFlowAUC{} (event flow); the informed model A gives \nBayAExistAUC{}, \nBayACardAUC{} and \nBayAFlowAUC{} on
the same events (Fig.~\ref{fig:bayes-flags}). The mild separation is therefore a property of 2p2h inputs, not
of blindness, and it agrees with Sections~\ref{sec:holdout}: 2p2h final states lie inside the training support,
model B reproduces them as well as A does, and the model's own uncertainty says so.

\paragraph{Can the flag detect anything?} A flag that did not fire proves nothing unless it is shown to fire when it
should. The positive control of Fig.~\ref{fig:bayes-pc} takes the same control events and perturbs them into
regions the training data cannot contain, then histograms the same three variances. Moving the vertex far upstream raises the epistemic variance
of the reconstruction flag by a factor \nPCVtxExistRatio{} (detection AUC \nPCVtxExistAUC{}), of the multiplicity
logits by \nPCVtxCardRatio{} (\nPCVtxCardAUC{}) and of the event-flow sample by \nPCVtxFlowRatio{} (\nPCVtxFlowAUC{}).
Scaling all hadron momenta by four or tripling the hadron tokens changes the variances by only
\nPCPfourExistRatio{} and \nPCTokthreeExistRatio{} (AUC \nPCPfourExistAUC{}, \nPCTokthreeExistAUC{}). The flag
responds to an unfamiliar context but hardly to unfamiliar kinematics or multiplicity, because the set encoder
maps those into feature regions it has seen: momenta enter through a saturating $\mathrm{asinh}$ and the global
token is dominated by the context. This is the known limitation of last-layer methods, which see novelty only
through the last-layer features, and it applies to any model of this kind, including the variational Bayesian
last-layer model used elsewhere in this programme. It also indicates the remedy for kinematic extrapolation:
Bayesian treatment deeper in the network, or an input-space novelty score alongside the last-layer one.

\paragraph{Is the uncertainty calibrated?} Two kinds of binned comparison with the open dataset are made, on
the held-out 2p2h test events (\nBayBnOOD{} events) and on a control sample of in-distribution test events
(\nBayBnCtl{} events). The first binned observables \emph{against a truth variable}: the reconstruction probability and
the mean prong count versus the true hadron count, the mean recoil, non-vertex and isolated-blob energies versus
the true hadronic kinetic energy, and the mean muon momentum ratio and angle difference versus the true muon
momentum, in 12 to 16 bins each. The second bins the observables \emph{in themselves}: the marginal histograms of
the prong count, the three energies, the muon momentum ratio and the muon angle difference, as the fraction of
reconstructed events per bin, in 9 to 40 bins. The distinction matters for what the test can see. A conditional
mean carries the full spread of the observable within the truth bin as its statistical error, which is large; a
bin fraction carries only a binomial error, which is small. The first test therefore asks mainly whether the
surrogate agrees with the open dataset within statistics, the second whether the epistemic band is the right
size for the disagreement that remains once statistics are no longer limiting.

In both cases $K=8$ posterior weight draws of the flag, multiplicity and event-flow output layers are made, each
followed by a fresh generation of the sample. The across-draw variance of a binned prediction contains two parts,
the epistemic variance from the re-drawn weights and the surrogate's own sampling noise in that bin (fresh noise
per draw); the latter is estimated within each draw and subtracted, and since the prediction compared is the mean
over the $K$ draws, only $1/K$ of it survives. The pull against the open dataset is the difference divided by the
epistemic variance, the surrogate's sampling variance over $K$, and the open dataset's statistical variance, in
quadrature. (A first version of this test put the full across-draw spread in the denominator, which over-counts
the surrogate's sampling noise by nearly a factor $K$ and produced pull widths of about 0.7; the decomposition
above removes that bias.) Both models are shown, since the claim to establish concerns the \emph{blind} model on
the mode it never saw. Table~\ref{tab:bayes-bins} lists all bins explicitly; a bin enters a pull distribution only
if it holds at least 30 events in both the open dataset and the surrogate sample.

\emph{Conditional means} (Fig.~\ref{fig:bayes-cal}, individual observables in Section~\ref{sec:bayes-binned}).
For model B on the held-out 2p2h events the pull RMS over \nBayBOODBins{} bins is \nBayBOODPullRMS{} with
\nBayBOODWithin{} of bins within $|$pull$| < 2$ (95\% expected for a unit normal); on the control sample
\nBayBCtlPullRMS{} and \nBayBCtlWithin{}; model A gives \nBayAOODPullRMS{} and \nBayACtlPullRMS{}. Without the
epistemic term the RMS would be \nBayBOODPullRMSnoEpi{} and \nBayBCtlPullRMSnoEpi{}: the test is dominated by the
open dataset's statistical error, as the error bars in Figs.~\ref{fig:bb-eff}--\ref{fig:bb-dthx} make plain, and
it establishes agreement within statistics rather than the size of the epistemic band. In the median bin the
epistemic estimate is consistent with zero; the ratio of epistemic to statistical error exceeds one half in
\nBayBOODEpiStatFracHalf{} (2p2h) and \nBayBCtlEpiStatFracHalf{} (control) of the bins for model B, at the
sparsely populated ends of the ranges.

\emph{Marginal histograms} (Fig.~\ref{fig:bayes-cal-marg}, individual observables in
Section~\ref{sec:bayes-binned}). Here the per-bin statistical error is small enough that percent-level
imperfections of the surrogate are resolved, and the pulls widen accordingly: for model B the RMS is
\nBayBOODMargPullRMS{} over \nBayBOODMargBins{} bins on held-out 2p2h (\nBayBOODMargWithin{} within two) and
\nBayBCtlMargPullRMS{} over \nBayBCtlMargBins{} bins on the control (\nBayBCtlMargWithin{}), with the largest
single pull near \nBayBCtlMargMaxPull{}; model A gives \nBayAOODMargPullRMS{} and \nBayACtlMargPullRMS{}. The
median relative difference between the surrogate's and the open dataset's bin fractions is
\nBayBCtlMargRelDiffMedian{} on the control and \nBayBOODMargRelDiffMedian{} on 2p2h. The pulls are largest for the
two calorimetric quantities whose positive part begins at a hard threshold, the isolated-blob energy (a third of
reconstructed events are exactly zero, the continuum starts near 6\,MeV) and the non-vertex energy within
100\,mm (six percent zero): the zero populations themselves are exact, but a continuous flow smooths a hard lower
edge upward, so the first continuum bins, 7 to 12\,MeV for the blobs, are under-populated by ten to twenty
percent and the bins from 20 to 60\,MeV over-populated by twenty to fifty percent (Figs.~\ref{fig:bm-blobs},
\ref{fig:bm-nv100}; Section~\ref{sec:zero} gives the history and the proposed cure). The recoil shows wiggles of
three to five percent in its bulk (Fig.~\ref{fig:bm-recoil}), the muon observables are within a few percent
everywhere, and the prong-count fractions agree to a few per mille, with pulls near one.
The epistemic band is far below these differences, with the across-draw spread consistent with sampling noise in
the median bin. The reading is unambiguous: the
Bayesian last layers quantify the uncertainty of the output weights given the training set, which after two
million events is small, and they do \emph{not} quantify the model's residual imperfection, which is at the
percent level on marginal distributions and is the dominant model-side error wherever the open dataset's
statistics are good enough to see it. The epistemic uncertainty is a floor on the model error, not its total,
and it should be used as the flag and the parameter-uncertainty component that it is. The control sample being
at least as far from unit pulls as the held-out 2p2h sample, and the two models agreeing on the 2p2h events, is
the same conclusion as Section~\ref{sec:holdout} reached from the classifier: the blind model's residual on 2p2h
is the model's residual, not the blindness.

\paragraph{Misfit, not shift.} The split is by subrun, so the test subruns are different data-taking periods from
the training ones, and the residual above could in principle be a run-to-run change of the detector that the
vertex and target inputs do not describe. Repeating the marginal comparison on a control sample of
\nBayBnCtl{} events drawn from the \emph{training} split gives the same pulls: RMS \nBayBTrnMargPullRMS{} (B)
and \nBayATrnMargPullRMS{} (A) against \nBayBCtlMargPullRMS{} and \nBayACtlMargPullRMS{} on the test split,
with the same observables responsible. The output heads themselves are marginally exact on the training events,
as a converged cross-entropy with a free bias must be: the mean reconstruction probability and the mean
multiplicity softmax reproduce the empirical rates to better than a part in a thousand. The residual is
therefore the flow's approximation of the threshold edges and the recoil shape, present on the data the model
was trained on. It is not a shift between training and test data, and it is not something a post-hoc
correction could legitimately remove, since the training objective already minimised it on the same events.

\paragraph{A model-error floor.} The residual can be carried as an uncertainty rather than corrected. For each
marginal observable a per-bin relative floor is derived on the training-split control as the excess of the
squared surrogate-minus-data difference beyond the statistical and epistemic variances, smoothed over three
neighbouring bins, and added in quadrature to the epistemic and statistical terms of the pull. Derived on the
training split, it is applied to the test-split control, which it has never seen, and to the held-out 2p2h
events. Fig.~\ref{fig:bayes-floor} and Table~\ref{tab:floor} give the result. For model B the marginal pull RMS
on the test control goes from \nBayBCtlMargPullRMS{} to \nFloorBRmsCtlRMS{} (\nFloorBRmsCtlWithin{} within two)
and on 2p2h from \nBayBOODMargPullRMS{} to \nFloorBRmsOODRMS{} (\nFloorBRmsOODWithin{} within two); model A gives
\nFloorARmsCtlRMS{} and \nFloorARmsOODRMS{}. The floors are \nFloorBRmsRecoilPct{} for the recoil,
\nFloorBRmsMuPPct{} and \nFloorBRmsDthxPct{} for the muon, \nFloorBRmsNvtxPct{} for the non-vertex energy and
\nFloorBRmsBlobsPct{} for the isolated-blob energy. The one observable the floor does not fully cover on 2p2h is
the blob energy, at RMS \nFloorBRmsBlobsOOD{} (B) and \nFloorARmsBlobsOOD{} (A): 2p2h produces proton-neutron
pairs, neutrons make isolated blobs, and the 2p2h deviation is larger than the control's for both models, the
blind one slightly more so, which is the only place the blindness is visible. A conservative variant with the
floor scaled by 1.5 gives \nFloorBConsOODRMS{} overall on 2p2h and \nFloorBConsBlobsOOD{} for the blobs, at the
cost of over-covering the control at \nFloorBConsCtlRMS{}. Two qualifications apply. The floor is a
distribution-level object, a relative error on bin fractions per observable and region measured on the open
dataset, not a per-event width, and it does not grow outside the support; on 2p2h, a composition shift inside the
support, the floor does the work and the epistemic term is negligible. Whether the epistemic term grows enough
where the support actually thins is the question an out-of-support holdout has to answer, and the positive
control above says the last layer reacts to context, not to kinematics.

\paragraph{Uncertainties in the output file.} The ntuple writer attaches the epistemic uncertainties to every
written event. Two kinds are stored. Analytic, in the model's standardised units and needing no extra sampling:
the standard deviation of the three flag logits (\texttt{surrogate\_epi\_logit\_*}), the mean variance of the
multiplicity logits, and the event-flow flag (the sum of the nine first-order variances along the nominal
trajectory, \texttt{surrogate\_epi\_flow\_flag}). And in physical units, from $K$ posterior weight draws regenerated
with the same base noise so that the draws differ only through the weights: the standard deviation of the muon
momentum and its components, of the reconstructed vertex, of the three generated energies, of the reconstruction,
MINOS and charge probabilities, and of the prong count (\texttt{surrogate\_epi\_muon\_P}, \texttt{\_vtx\_z},
\texttt{\_recoil\_E}, \texttt{\_p\_reco\_exists}, \texttt{\_n\_prongs}, \ldots), with $K$ recorded in
\texttt{surrogate\_epi\_draws}. $K$ is a free parameter; a standard deviation estimated from $K$ draws has a
relative precision of about $1/\sqrt{2(K-1)}$, 27\% at $K=8$ and 18\% at the default $K=16$, at a cost of $K$
generation passes. Typical values on in-distribution events are an epistemic spread of 0.2\% on the muon
momentum, 0.5\% on the recoil energy and 0.003 on the reconstruction probability, i.e.\ small compared with the
aleatoric spread of the samples, which is the expected situation inside the training support.

\section{Known limitations}
\label{sec:limits}

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Derived branches.} \texttt{recoil\_passivecorrected} equals $0.6669\times$\texttt{recoil\_E} for more
  than 15\% of events and a geometry-dependent ratio otherwise; \texttt{hadron\_recoil} equals
  $1.385\times$\texttt{recoil\_E} within a MeV for a large fraction. Both are filled from \texttt{recoil\_E} and
  a median ratio in 250\,mm bins of true $z$, which drops the event-by-event scatter of the calibration (16--84\%
  widths of about 3\% and 10\%). Analyses that depend on that scatter should use \texttt{recoil\_E}.
\item \textbf{No truth-matched prongs.} The tuple's per-prong truth match is used only to validate the training
  data. A per-prong pointer head over the particle tokens would provide it.
\item \textbf{Scope.} CC~$\nu_\mu$, ME FHC playlist 1A, true vertex $z \ge 4000$\,mm, one training seed.
  The holdout of Section~\ref{sec:holdout} tests a composition shift within covered support; extrapolation to
  final states absent from training is not tested.
\item \textbf{Hard lower edges.} The positive parts of the isolated-blob and non-vertex energies begin at a
  physical threshold of a few MeV, and the flow smooths that edge upward by a few MeV; the blob energy is the one
  marginal distribution whose pulls stay well above one and the one observable the model-error floor does not
  cover on 2p2h (Section~\ref{sec:zero}). A threshold-removing transform of the positive part is the proposed cure.
\item \textbf{Epistemic uncertainty.} The Bayesian last layers of Section~\ref{sec:bayes} quantify the
  uncertainty of the output weights and flag an unfamiliar context, but they do not flag unfamiliar kinematics or
  multiplicity, which the encoder normalises into familiar features, and they do not cover the model's residual
  imperfection, which is at the percent level on marginal distributions and exceeds the epistemic band wherever
  the open dataset's statistics resolve it. The written-out per-event uncertainties are therefore a lower bound
  on the model error; the distribution-level model-error floor of Section~\ref{sec:bayes} covers the residual
  on the control and on 2p2h except for the isolated-blob energy. An input-space novelty score or a Bayesian
  treatment deeper in the network would be needed for kinematic extrapolation.
\end{itemize}

\appendix
\section{Data conventions that had to be handled}
\label{app:conv}

Every one of the following was found because a closure classifier separated real from surrogate output, not
from one-dimensional plots. They are recorded here because any surrogate of this tuple will meet them.
(1)~All angle branches are in the beam frame, rotated about $x$ by $-0.05887$\,rad; momentum components are in
the detector frame. (2)~\texttt{muon\_qp} is $-9999.9$ when there is no MINOS match, so ``negative charge'' must be
defined as matched and negative. (3)~A prong with $P=1$, $E=0$ is a direction-only sentinel. (4)~Eight of 4.8
million reconstructed events have NaN or infinite muon momenta or vertices, one a vertex coordinate of order
$10^{14}$\,mm. (5)~The two calibrated recoil branches are point masses in ratio to \texttt{recoil\_E}. (6)~The
reconstructed vertex $z$ is quantised to plane positions for \nSnapReal{} of events, and the plane chosen depends on the
true $z$'s phase within the 22\,mm cycle. (7)~Broad tails (unmatched muons, vertex residuals) must be compressed,
not clipped: clipping at $\pm 12$ robust $\sigma$ put 9--17\% of those variables on the clip boundary. (8)~Dropping
neutrons or soft protons from the input is not harmless: 2p2h final states were the mode where their
calorimetric activity and fake prongs showed up as an unexplained residual (Section~\ref{sec:inputdef}).
